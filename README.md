# The Chip of Theseus
**Human-AI Coupling and the Erosion of Legal Individuation**

Deborah B. Glosser
Western Washington University, Department of Engineering and Design
glossed@wwu.edu

---

## Abstract

A contract binds the party who assented. A ballot records one voter's judgment. An agent's acts bind one principal. Where law has needed a person who is not a single human being, it has said so explicitly: corporate personhood, for example, is a fiction adopted deliberately and revisable at will. Everywhere else the count came from biology – one person, one judgment – and none of these rules asks how many judgments produced an act, because the question has never arisen. AI models worn continuously, answering in the rhythm of ordinary speech, break the assumption that one person produces one judgment. The clearest remaining barrier is latency, and it is commercial rather than surgical. A wearer who hears a model speak knows that a model spoke. What is not known is whether the judgment is still marked as the model's by the time she acts on it: source memory decays faster than the content it carries, and no one has tested how that behaves at conversational speed.

This Article separates three questions that are easily conflated: whether the coupled person can perceive that a judgment was furnished (source monitoring); whether anyone else can establish that coupling occurred (coupling detection); and whether any party can establish that a given judgment was the model's (authorship attribution). The first two admit of answers. The third does not – attribution requires knowing what the person would have decided unaided, and no record of an actual interaction contains that counterfactual. Voting, contract, and agency law each require precisely that input. Undue influence, the doctrine built for this case, cannot void the resulting contract: coupling leaves no outward sign, so the counterparty deals in good faith. Elections aggregate ballots as independent judgments, while coupling can supply correlation from a handful of providers – and federal standing doctrine offers no route to raise it.

The Article proposes instruments that operate on the answerable questions instead: latency floors, so that model suggestions arrive above the threshold at which their origin can be appraised; a contemporaneous indicator of coupling, which supplies an observable predicate to which legislation could attach notice, presumptions, or rescission rights; and rules allocating the unavailable counterfactual by presumption. Logging and retention are considered and rejected on privacy grounds.

**Keywords:** AI governance, human-AI interaction, wearable AI, legal individuation, source monitoring, election integrity, legal personhood, latency
